### What the ledger shows

The single most important fact about this book's evidence base is its composition. Of the forty-three items above, the overwhelming majority are `case` and `story` entries — named patients (Jacob, David, Chris, Michael, Maria, Delilah, Lori, Muhammad) and the author's own confessions (romance-novel reading, the chocolate bunnies) — with a minority of neuroscience `study` items and a thin layer of aggregate `data` and cited `authority`. In other words, *Dopamine Nation* is built primarily on clinical anecdote, structured around the DOPAMINE mnemonic and the pleasure-pain balance metaphor, and only secondarily on experimental science. This is by design: the introduction explicitly announces that neuroscience alone is insufficient and that the lived experience of people with addiction must supply the practical wisdom the mechanism cannot [ch01 ¶11]. The evidentiary profile is the argument's method made visible.

The neuroscience that does appear tends to occupy the foundational rows, and it clusters in the mechanism chapters. Chapter 3 (ch04) carries the heaviest experimental load — Volkow's imaging of a dopamine-deficit reward region [ch04 ¶32], the Linnet gambling study showing craving peaks at maximum uncertainty [ch04 ¶55], and the dopamine-release rank ordering [ch04 ¶13] — because that chapter is where the book earns the right to talk about a physical "balance" at all. Chapter 7 (ch08) reprises the pattern in reverse, using the Prague cold-water immersion [ch08 ¶23], Solomon's shocked dogs [ch08 ¶37], and the Sprenger naloxone-blocked analgesia study [ch08 ¶74] to argue that pain recruits a compensatory pleasure response. These are the rows on which the book's causal claims genuinely rest; strip them out and the balance metaphor reverts to a figure of speech.

The aggregate `data` items do real rhetorical work in the diagnostic chapters. The Prohibition "decreased by half" figure [ch02 ¶85] is the empirical spine of the access-as-risk claim; the psychiatric-drug prevalence figure [ch03 ¶46], the cross-country happiness and pain surveys [ch03 ¶91], and the four-country funding-versus-prevalence data [ch07 ¶59] together supply the "more comfort, more misery" paradox that motivates the whole project. All four are explicitly correlational, and the packets flag them as such — the funding data even notes controls for poverty and trauma [ch07 ¶59]. The book does not claim these figures prove causation; it uses them to establish that a puzzle exists, then reaches for mechanism and anecdote to explain it.

Two structural features of the ledger deserve emphasis. First, several of the most quotable, memorable items are the *weakest* by evidentiary standard: the smartphone-as-hypodermic-needle line [ch01 ¶6], the "poisons become vitamins" aphorism [ch03 ¶73], the "sensory rich and causal poor" remark [ch05 ¶31], and the closing "leads to pain" maxim [ch11 ¶20] are rhetorical or authority items, not demonstrations. They stick in memory precisely because they compress rather than prove. Second, the animal studies (the abstinence-then-binge rats [ch05 ¶115], Ahmed and Koob's cocaine rats [ch06 ¶74], Solomon's dogs [ch08 ¶37]) are load-bearing for claims about human moderation and hormesis, yet the packets themselves note the species gap — the binge-rat caveat even concedes the driver may be genetics rather than the drug [ch05 ¶115]. The book leans on animal models to license behavioral prescriptions for people.

**The central caveat: reliance on self-selected clinical stories.** The dominant evidence type here is the patient case, and every such row carries the same structural limitation, which the packets record item by item: these are single, self-reported narratives drawn from the practice of one clinician who chose which patients to write about and how to frame them. There is no control group, no denominator, and no way to know how many patients tried a dopamine fast and did not experience Delilah's "my anxiety is gone. Gone!" moment [ch05 ¶91], or embraced honesty without Maria's restored freedom [ch09 ¶20], or immersed themselves like Muhammad without their cravings decreasing [ch11 ¶10]. The cases that appear are, by definition, the ones that illustrate the thesis; failures and non-responders are largely absent except where the author uses them to make a further point (the roughly 20 percent of fasters who do not improve, cited to argue those patients have a co-occurring disorder). Some cases are also second-hand — the six-year-old reaches the reader through Detective Dutto [ch02 ¶128], the AA mentor's de-shaming is recalled rather than observed [ch10 ¶57] — adding a further layer of selection and reconstruction. Even the strongest cases carry unresolved tails the ledger records: Chris's buprenorphine "light switch" is also a lifelong dependence [ch07 ¶48], and Drake's redemptive guilty plea is one the author herself concedes was shaped by race and class privilege [ch09 ¶182]. A reader should therefore treat the clinical stories as vivid existence proofs and mechanism illustrations — this can happen, and here is what it looks like — rather than as evidence of typical or reliable outcomes. The book's persuasive power comes from the accumulation of concordant anecdotes around a coherent neurobiological frame; its evidentiary limit is that concordant anecdotes, however many, cannot establish base rates, and the sample was assembled by the same person advancing the claim.
