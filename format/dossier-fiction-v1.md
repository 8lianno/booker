# Fiction Dossier Format v1 — Output Contract

This contract defines the output structure for **Fiction** book dossiers produced by `booker`. It ensures that fiction books are analyzed through narrative arc, character transformations, worldbuilding, symbolic systems, and literary subtext—rather than dry non-fiction argument chains.

## Purpose

A reader who has not read the novel will understand:
1. The core premise, emotional core, and central dramatic question.
2. The plot mechanics, major acts, and turning points.
3. Character arcs, motivations, internal/external conflicts, and transformations.
4. Setting, worldbuilding, and environmental atmosphere.
5. Symbolic motifs, themes, allegories, and underlying subtext.
6. A chapter-by-chapter narrative walkthrough that captures the storytelling drive.
7. Critical literary context and enduring themes.
8. Active recall hooks and character/plot memory bank.

---

## The 10 Mandatory Sections

### 1. Executive Orientation & Narrative Premise
- **Premise & Logline**: High-concept summary of the story.
- **Central Dramatic Question**: The primary emotional or narrative conflict driving the protagonist.
- **Tone & Mood Palette**: Descriptive analysis of authorial style, voice, and atmospheric quality.

### 2. Plot Mechanics, Act Structure & Turning Points Matrix
- **3-Act / 5-Act Structural Mapping**: Setup, inciting incident, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution.
- **Turning Points Table**:
  `| Act / Part | Major Event | Catalyst | Emotional / Structural Shift | Anchor |`

### 3. Character Transformation & Arc Matrix
- Detailed profiles for primary and key secondary characters:
  - **Goal / Desire** (What they think they want)
  - **Need / Flaw** (Internal weakness or lie they believe)
  - **Transformation Curve** (Starting state → Climax test → Final resolution state)
  - **Key Relationships & Foils**

### 4. Worldbuilding, Lore & Setting Dynamics
- **Setting as Character**: How time, place, social hierarchy, and environment influence the plot.
- **Rules of the World**: Magic systems, societal norms, historical backdrop, or technological rules.

### 5. Symbolic Motifs, Themes & Subtext System
- **Core Themes**: Central philosophical or human questions explored.
- **Motifs & Recurring Symbols**: Objects, phrases, colors, or weather patterns carrying deeper meaning.
- **Subtext & Unsaid Dynamics**: What lies beneath the dialogue and surface interactions.

### 6. Chapter-by-Chapter Narrative Journey
- For each chapter / section:
  - **Narrative Beat**: Concise story advancement.
  - **Character Movements**: Shifts in status, knowledge, or emotional state.
  - **Key Quote & Scene**: Load-bearing moment.
  - **Connection**: How it sets up subsequent chapters.

### 7. Pacing, Narrative Perspective & Craft Analysis
- **Point of View (POV)**: 1st person, 3rd limited, omniscient, unreliable narrator nuances.
- **Pacing & Tension Curve**: How tension is built, released, or sustained.

### 8. Critical Subtext & Literary Impact
- **Historical & Cultural Context**: When it was written and what it responds to.
- **Enduring Relevance**: Why this story resonates across time.

### 9. Practical & Emotional Synthesis
- Human insights, moral dilemmas, and reader reflections.

### 10. Memory Hooks & Character/Plot Recall Bank
- 15 compressed plot memory hooks.
- 15 character & scene recall Q&A items.
